02 — The Physics of AI

Data gravity decides where agents can actually live

In 2010, Dave McCrory coined "data gravity": as data accumulates, it becomes harder to move — services and applications get pulled toward the data, not the other way around. For AI agents, that pull is stronger than ever. Agents don't just read data — they reason over it continuously, hold context across sessions, and synthesize across formats. The platform with the richest, most interconnected, most deeply indexed data doesn't just compete. It wins by default.

A moat no connector can replicateMeet the librarian and the runner ↓

The librarian, not the runner

Indexed data behaves like a research librarian who has read every book and cross-referenced them: "here are three relevant sources, and this one contradicts last week's proposal." An API or connector behaves like a runner: it fetches exactly the book you name, and nothing more.

The Semantic Index

The Librarian
  • Conceptual, vector-based matching — not keyword search
  • Joins email, chat, docs, and CRM in one retrieval
  • Local-cache speed — milliseconds, not network round-trips
  • Natively inherits identity and permissions
  • Feeds a persistent, growing memory over time
  • Enables proactive nudges, not just answers on demand

API / MCP Connector

The Runner
  • Exact-query parameters only — no conceptual match
  • Serial, single-purpose calls that don't compound
  • Subject to network latency, rate limits, and throttling
  • Often relies on broad service accounts
  • Ephemeral — used once, then gone
  • Purely reactive — fires only when explicitly called

Connectors are excellent for transactional actions. They are structurally limited for intelligence and reasoning — and that gap compounds rather than cancels out.

11 / Overview · Human-led by design

Choose how much AI may do.

Assistance, delegation, and execution are different operating choices. More autonomy is not automatically more value.

01

Assist

A person directs the work. AI drafts, summarizes, or answers; the person reviews and decides.

Boundary: support the task without transferring decision authority.

02

Delegate

A person assigns defined tasks to agents and coordinates their contributions.

Boundary: specify the outcome, permitted tools, and review checkpoints.

03

Execute within bounds

Agents carry out a defined process. People set approval gates, oversee results, and handle exceptions.

Boundary: constrain actions, define stop conditions, and retain a fallback.

Earn greater autonomy: expand only when measured reliability, risk controls, and operational ownership justify it.

People retain accountability at every levelMake trust operational ↓

11 / Overview · Risk and readiness

Make trust a condition of investment.

A connected platform does not remove risk. Agree on safeguards and accountable owners before expanding access or autonomy.

Reliable outputs

Use approved knowledge, test representative cases, and verify evidence before consequential decisions.

Owners: business owner + CTO.

Privacy & access

Minimize sensitive data, review permissions, and test what different user roles can retrieve.

Owners: CIO + data and privacy leads.

Safe actions

Treat retrieved content as untrusted. Restrict tools and privileges; require approval for high-impact actions.

Owners: CTO + CISO + process owner.

Defensible decisions

Record decisions and approvals, define retention, and review regulated uses with Legal.

Owners: business owner + compliance.

Adoption & judgment

Co-design with users, teach verification, and address weak trust or overreliance through feedback.

Owners: COO + function leader + HR.

Cost & resilience

Set budgets, track failures, and maintain fallback, rollback, and incident procedures.

Owners: CIO + COO + Finance.

13 / Execution · A testable investment

Run a bounded pilot with real work.

Choose one meaningful constraint, one accountable owner, and a defined user group. Agree the review point and success thresholds before launch.

Before launch

Write the outcome charter

Define the workflow, users, baseline, expected benefit, budget, and pilot duration.

Output: an outcome charter and baseline scorecard.

Set the boundary

Decide what AI may do

Approve knowledge, tools, access, and permitted actions. Specify human gates, stop conditions, and fallback.

Output: an access plan, evaluation set, and control checklist.

In the workflow

Learn with the users

Redesign handoffs, teach verification, test representative cases, and capture failures and feedback.

Output: a pilot readout on outcomes, quality, adoption, and full cost.

Keep the pilot reversible. Route uncertain or consequential actions to a named person, and retain a workable non-AI path.

Start narrow enough to learn—and meaningful enough to matterJudge value against the baseline ↓

13 / Execution · Evidence before expansion

Measure outcomes, not AI activity.

Compare the pilot with its baseline. Separate task time saved from realized business value, and account for quality, risk, and the full cost of operation.

Business result

Did performance improve?

Track cycle time, quality, customer or employee experience, and the workflow's business outcome.

Reliability & control

Was the result trustworthy?

Track errors, rework, escalations, approval failures, and the quality of exception handling.

Adoption & capacity

Did work actually change?

Track sustained use, user feedback, changed handoffs, and where freed capacity was redeployed.

Full economics

Was it worth the cost?

Include licenses, usage, integration, data preparation, human review, training, support, and monitoring.

Use cost per successful task alongside business outcomes. A faster draft is not a saving if review, failures, or operating costs absorb the benefit.

Finance and the business owner validate the value caseMake the investment decision ↓

13 / Execution · The leadership decision

Scale. Revise. Or stop.

Review the evidence against the thresholds agreed in advance. Make the next investment a deliberate decision—not an automatic extension of the pilot.

Scale when the case holds

Fund the operating model

Outcomes, reliability, adoption, and economics meet the agreed bar. Controls and service ownership are ready.

Next: expand in stages, reuse patterns, and re-evaluate new access or autonomy.

Revise when the gap is fixable

Change the design

There is a credible value case, but data, handoffs, controls, or user experience need improvement.

Next: narrow scope, fix the constraint, and retest against a defined review point.

Stop when the case fails

Protect the investment

Benefits do not justify full cost, unacceptable risk remains, or the workflow does not improve.

Next: retire the pilot safely, preserve lessons, and redirect effort.

The goal is not more AI in more places. It is better business outcomes through connected work, useful context, and accountable execution.
