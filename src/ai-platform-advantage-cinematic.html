The librarian, not the runner

Connectors act. Indexing understands.

The Semantic Index

📚 The Librarian

  • Conceptual, vector-based matching, not keyword search
  • Joins email, chat, docs and CRM in one retrieval
  • Local-cache speed: milliseconds, not network round-trips
  • Natively inherits identity and permissions
  • Feeds a persistent, growing memory over time
  • Enables proactive nudges, not just answers on demand
VS
API / MCP Connector

🏃 The Runner

  • Exact-query parameters only, no conceptual match
  • Serial, single-purpose calls that don't compound
  • Subject to network latency, rate limits and throttling
  • Often relies on broad service accounts
  • Ephemeral: used once, then gone
  • Purely reactive: fires only when explicitly called

Connectors are excellent for transactional actions. They are structurally limited for intelligence and reasoning, and that gap grows over time instead of closing.

Human-led by design

Choose how much AI may do

More autonomy is not automatically more value.

01

Assist

A person directs the work. AI drafts, summarizes or answers; the person reviews and decides.

Boundary: no transfer of decision authority
02

Delegate

A person assigns defined tasks to agents and coordinates their contributions.

Boundary: outcome, permitted tools, review checkpoints
03

Execute within bounds

Agents carry out a defined process. People set approval gates, oversee results and handle exceptions.

Boundary: constrained actions, stop conditions, fallback
Earn greater autonomy: expand only when measured reliability, risk controls and operational ownership justify it. People stay accountable at every level.

Evidence before expansion

Measure outcomes, not AI activity

Business result

Did performance improve?

Cycle time, quality, customer or employee experience, and the business outcome.

Reliability & control

Was it trustworthy?

Errors, rework, escalations, approval failures and exception handling.

Adoption & capacity

Did work change?

Sustained use, feedback, changed handoffs and where freed capacity went.

Full economics

Was it worth the cost?

Licenses, usage, integration, data prep, human review, training, support, monitoring.

Use cost per successful task alongside business outcomes. A faster draft is not a saving if review, failures or operating costs absorb the benefit.
The leadership decision

Scale. Revise. Or stop.

Scale

Fund the operating model

Outcomes, reliability, adoption and economics meet the agreed bar. Controls and service ownership are ready.

Next: expand in stages, reuse patterns, re-evaluate new access or autonomy.
Revise

Change the design

A credible value case, but data, handoffs, controls or user experience need work.

Next: narrow scope, fix the constraint, retest at a defined review point.
Stop

Protect the investment

Benefits don't justify full cost, unacceptable risk remains, or the workflow doesn't improve.

Next: retire safely, preserve lessons, redirect effort.
The goal is not more AI in more places. It is better business outcomes through connected work, useful context and accountable execution.
